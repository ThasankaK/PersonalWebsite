Sudoku solver

Upload a photo of a Sudoku puzzle. It finds the grid, reads the digits with a CNN I trained, and solves the rest by backtracking. Everything runs in your browser — the image is never uploaded anywhere. Source.

Choose a Sudoku image, then send it to the backend.

How it works

  1. Grayscale, a 5×5 Gaussian blur, then an adaptive threshold (Gaussian, block 11, C 2) — the same parameters as the Python version.
  2. Find external contours, keep the largest four-sided one above 50px² using approxPolyDP at 2% of the perimeter. That's the puzzle border.
  3. Order those four corners by the sum and difference of their coordinates, then warp the quadrilateral to a flat 450×450 square.
  4. Split into 81 cells, trim 4px of border off each, resize to 32×32, normalize, and run all 81 through the CNN in one batch. Predictions under 20% confidence are treated as empty.
  5. Check the read board for duplicate digits in any row, column, or box — a single misread creates a contradiction, and it's better to point at it than to fail silently.
  6. Solve by backtracking, capped at two million steps so a bad read can never hang the tab.
  7. Draw the found digits onto the flat grid, warp them back through the inverse transform, and blend over your original image.
